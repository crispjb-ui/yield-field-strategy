How to stake and build
Allow staking once
The first stake asks the wallet to let the game hold your staked Deeds: “Your first stake asks
you to allow the staking contract once, then to stake”. 
Verified
Stake
“Staking is free. A staked deed makes Fertilizer once it has a Fert Deck.” Only gas is paid. The Deed
is held for staking and stays yours. 
Verified
Build a Fert Deck
0.01 ETH a deck, “one signature for all of them”. Staking and building can be done in one
transaction, paying 0.01 ETH × number of Deeds. The game checks the fee again just
before sending; if it changed: “The fee changed, review again. Nothing was built.”
Verified
Claim
Claimed Fertilizer goes straight to your Bag, with no wallet signature: “No wallet needed: the
Fertilizer goes straight to your Bag.” Only whole units are claimed. 
Verified
The build fee is a setting the team can change; the docs call it “0.01 ETH at launch”.
May change
 A fee of 0 would mean building is closed (“Fert Deck building isn’t
open yet”). 
Verified

one structure

The Root Cellar
The 
Root Cellar
 is the ETH that backs the Founding Deeds. Every Fert Deck build fee goes into it,
and every Deed, staked or not, owns a share of it set by its Fertility. The only way to collect a
share is to 
burn

Verified
Root Cellar of a Deed = backing × F / 19,773
Backing is the ETH held for the Deeds, F is the Deed’s Fertility and 19,773 is the total
Fertility of all 3,333 Deeds. The game’s own burn quote returns exactly this.
Verified
The 19,773 counts 
every
 Deed, including the ones that are not staked. An unstaked Deed’s share
keeps growing like any other; it only has to be staked before it can be burned.
Verified
Today’s values
The backing is the whole ETH balance held for the Deeds, which so far equals every Fert Deck build
fee paid (0.01 ETH each). It is shared by Fertility: a Deed’s Root Cellar is backing × F ÷ total
Fertility.

 The game does not show it on screen yet; the Deed panel

What the release lock does.
 Anything that enters the lock pays out over about a day: 50 % after 8

share of the fees paid by those who claimed earlier.

If the Silo opens or closes between review and sending, the burn stops and asks for a new review: “The

Why a burn does not change the backing per point
A burned Deed takes out exactly its share, backing × F / total, and its Fertility leaves the total
with it. What remains per point is the same:
before: backing / total
after: (backing − backing × F / total) / (total − F) = backing / total
So a burn does not raise or lower anyone else’s Root Cellar. What changes is the future: every later
inflow is divided among fewer Fertility points. The docs put it as “each burned deed means future fees
are shared among fewer deeds”.

shared over 19,764 points instead of 19,773.

Use it on long crops
A Fertilizer always saves a quarter of the crop’s own grow time, so it saves the most on the longest crops: 12 times more on a Bronze Mythic (3 h) than on a Diamond Common (15 min). It matters most when your 25 slots are the limit: two Fertilizers make a slot cycle twice as fast.

Worked examples
A late Fertilizer ripens at once.
 A Heartbeet (10 h) planted at 08:00 is due at 18:00. At 16:00 it has 2 h left, less than a quarter of 10 h. One Fertilizer moves the ripe time to 08:00 + 7 h 30 m = 15:30, which is already past, so it is ripe at once.
Two Fertilizers early.
 A Swirlchard (12 h) planted at 00:00 with two Fertilizers right away ripens at 06:00 instead of 12:00. The same slot can take a second seed the same morning.
Earlier is better, but never wasted.
 The cut is always of the total time, so with two Fertilizers a crop can never ripen before planted + 50 %. Applied early, both cuts count in full. Applied late, the second one ripens the crop at once at the moment it is used: a Swirlchard fertilized once at planting (due at 9 h) and again at 8 h ripens at 8 h, not at 6 h.
Fertilizer changes time, not value: a fertilized crop is worth exactly the same. What changes is how many harvests a slot makes in a day, and every harvest is a Truffle roll (below).

=================== fertilizer_exporting-and-trading.txt

Exporting and trading
Exporting
 turns Fertilizer in your Bag into an onchain token in your wallet, so it can be sent or traded on OpenSea like any other item. 
Importing
 turns it back. Exporting is rationed: a per-wallet limit, a slowdown of your own decks when you export a lot, and a network-wide daily cap. This page gives each rule with its exact formula, then the OpenSea collection: its royalty, its pricing and how much Fertilizer exists onchain.
Export and import
Export: Bag → wallet
You pick an amount. The game reserves it in your Bag (“They’re reserved from your Bag now and land in your wallet as tokens.”) and your wallet confirms the export, which mints the Fertilizer as a 
Yield Fields Items
 token (an ERC-1155 item) on Robinhood Chain. 
Verified
Use it onchain
The token can be sent to any wallet or traded on OpenSea (see 
Trading on OpenSea
 below). A token in your wallet does nothing in the game. 
Verified
Import: wallet → Bag
Your wallet confirms the import, the tokens are burned, and the game credits them to your Bag. Only then can they be used on crops. 
Verified
If you cancel in your wallet, “Your Fertilizer stays reserved: try again from Pending”. 
Verified
